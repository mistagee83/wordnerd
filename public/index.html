From a text to a game in a few minutes

  1. 1

    Give it a text

    Paste a reading text, or add up to four pages: PDFs, photos, or screenshots pasted with Ctrl+V. Choose the level, the language for the translations and, if you like, a focus such as phrasal verbs.

  2. 2

    Check the words

    About half a minute later you have the list: the longer the text, the more words, up to 40. Every word comes with a translation, a simple English definition and an example sentence. Fix what you don't like and play the games yourself before the class does.

  3. 3

    Open a room

    Choose the games and their difficulty, and if you like, a goal for the whole class. Students type the six-letter code or scan the QR code and pick a nickname. They don't need an account or an app.

  4. 4

    Watch it live

    Students play at their own pace and collect up to three stars per game. You see everyone's stars and the words the class found hardest.

Eight games from one list

All eight games use the same words. Students match them, spell them and put them into sentences. Everyone in a room gets the same puzzle.

  • Memory

    Turn the cards and find the pairs: each word with its translation or its definition.

  • Matching

    Drag each word onto its meaning, or tap the word, then the meaning.

  • Word search

    Find the words in the letter grid. The clues can be the words, their translations or definitions.

  • Crossword

    Solve the rows from definitions or translations; the yellow column spells a secret word.

  • Hangman

    Guess the word letter by letter before the balloons pop.

  • Gapfill

    Put the words into a short new text written with them, with a few extra words to make it harder.

  • Drowning

    Type the word for each prompt before the telephone box fills with water.

  • Bridge

    Choose the word that fits a new example sentence. Every right word lays a plank; a wrong one falls into the river.

See what was hard

While the room is open, your screen shows who has joined and how many stars each student has in each game. It updates by itself.

Under the table, WordNerd lists the words the class missed on their first try, so you know what to go over in the next lesson.

The details

  • Any text you teach

    A coursebook unit, an article, song lyrics: up to about 2,500 words.

  • Translations in 30 languages

    Definitions are in English, translations in your students' language.

  • Difficulty you choose

    Each game has its own settings: grid size, word directions, clue type, lives, speed.

  • Three stars per game

    Students can play a game again and their best try counts. The words they missed on the first try still show up for you.

  • A goal for the whole class

    If you like, set a class goal: a share of all the stars. Everyone sees one progress bar for the class, never each other's stars.

  • Your library

    Every list you make stays in your library. Open a new room from it next week or next year.

  • Students stay anonymous

    They type a code and a nickname, nothing else. Only the teacher's side uses AI.

Questions

What do my students need?

A phone, tablet or computer with a web browser. They open the WordNerd address, type the code (or scan the QR code) and choose a nickname. They don't sign up or give an e-mail address.

Which languages does it work with?

The texts and the games are in English for now. The translations can be in 30 languages.

How long does a room stay open?

Until you close it, for at most seven days, so students can also play at home. Without a Hub pass the results of a closed room are kept for 30 days; with a Hub pass they stay in your History.

What happens to the text I upload?

It is only used to make the word list. Uploaded files are not stored; the list keeps the words, their translations, definitions and example sentences.

Can I use my own word list?

WordNerd starts from a text, because the games need example sentences and a gapfill text. Once a list is made, you can add your own words to it by hand.
