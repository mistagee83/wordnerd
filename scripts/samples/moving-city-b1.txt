A New Start in a Big City

When Maya was sixteen, her mother got a new job and the family had to move from a quiet village to a busy city. At first, Maya was furious. She did not want to leave her friends, her school or the little café where she worked at weekends. "Why do we always have to do what other people decide?" she complained.

The first weeks were difficult. The flat was smaller than their old house, and the neighbours were noisy. Maya missed the fresh air and the silence of the countryside. On the bus to her new school, she felt lonely and invisible among hundreds of strangers.

Things started to change when her teacher asked her to join the school newspaper. Maya was shy, but she agreed to give it a try. Her first task was to interview people at a local street market. To her surprise, the stallholders were friendly and talkative. An old man who sold second-hand books told her about the history of the neighbourhood, and a young woman who ran a vegetable stall gave her advice about cheap places to eat.

Slowly, Maya got used to the rhythm of the city. She learned how to get around on the underground, she discovered a park where she could read in peace, and she made two close friends at the newspaper. She also realised that city life had advantages: there were concerts, museums and sports clubs, and she could choose from dozens of activities.

A year later, Maya visited her village again. It was lovely to see her old friends, but she noticed that she had changed. She was more confident and more independent, and she was no longer afraid of new situations. On the train back, she looked forward to getting home – and for the first time, home meant the city.
